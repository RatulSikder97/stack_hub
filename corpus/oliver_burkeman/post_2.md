# Why your morning routine is not the answer

I keep meeting people who are convinced that the right morning routine will fix the problem. The problem, in their telling, is that they are not yet the version of themselves they are meant to become. The routine is the bridge.

I have built that bridge a few times. It does not lead where you think it leads. It leads, mostly, to a slightly more disciplined version of the same person, with the same uncertainty about what to actually do with their day.

The structure of a morning is not where lives change. Lives change at the level of what you are willing to commit to and disappoint someone over. The five-step routine is, for most of us, a way of postponing that conversation by another season.

This is not an argument against routines. It is an argument against treating them as substitutes for harder decisions. By all means, drink water before coffee. Read for ten minutes. Then look at the work that has been waiting for you for six weeks and notice what it is asking, which is rarely a productivity question.

The honest version of self-improvement is small, slow, and uncomfortable in places that no app is going to reach.
