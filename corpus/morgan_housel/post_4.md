# The luxury of being wrong slowly

Most lessons people teach about money apply only if you have time to recover from being wrong. The faster the timeline, the less the wisdom helps.

This is why long horizons are quietly the most valuable financial asset a person can have. They are not glamorous. They do not come with a ticker. But they are the difference between a mistake that becomes a story and a mistake that becomes a permanent setback.

A young investor who is wrong about a sector for two years has time to update, ride a recovery, or simply move on. The same wrongness on a five-year timeline can be career-ending. Same decision, very different outcome, only because the runway differs.

I think about this when I see people optimize for high-conviction concentrated bets in their twenties. The math says this is not unreasonable, because they have time. The math is right and the practice is dangerous, because the people who make those bets often do not have the temperament to absorb the years where they are punished for being early.

The luxury of being wrong slowly is what compounding actually requires. The interest math is the easy part. The temperament math is what most people get wrong.
