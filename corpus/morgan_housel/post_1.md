# Two kinds of risk

There is the risk that something goes wrong tomorrow. And there is the risk that nothing goes wrong for years and the cost of the slow drift is invisible until it is enormous.

People are very good at managing the first kind. We buy insurance, build redundancies, run drills. We are bad at the second. The second kind of risk does not look like a risk while it is accumulating. It looks like things going fine.

A small example. A friend of mine ran his finances on the assumption that he would never have a year of unusually high expenses. He was right for a decade. The year he was wrong, he was very wrong, because the buffer he never built was the only buffer that would have helped.

The lesson I keep coming back to is that the boring habit — keeping a margin you do not seem to need — is almost always paid for by the year you wish you had it. People who survive long careers are usually not the smartest. They are the ones whose normal operating mode includes a quiet allowance for the rare, expensive year they cannot predict.

You do not get rewarded for this until you do. Then it is too late to start.
