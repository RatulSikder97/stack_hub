# Things that do not look like skill

Some of the most valuable behaviors in any career do not register as skills at the time. They look like personality, or luck, or restraint, until enough years have passed that you can see what they actually saved.

Patience looks like passivity until it turns out to be the reason you did not sell at the bottom. Saying no looks like a missed opportunity until it turns out to be the reason you were not exposed to the deal that took your competitors out. Reading widely looks like procrastination until your idiosyncratic angle on a problem becomes the asset.

This makes the people who quietly do these things hard to identify in real time. Their resumes do not advertise the deals they declined. Their performance reviews do not credit the ten years of not blowing up.

If I were trying to learn from someone, I would spend less time studying their wins and more time studying what they declined to do. The list of things a careful operator did not do is often the most concentrated form of their judgment that you can copy. It is also the only version of their advice that is actually free.

Skill, in long careers, is mostly a residue of refusals.
