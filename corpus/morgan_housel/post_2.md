# What incentives quietly do

If you want to predict behavior, do not study what people say. Study what they are paid to believe.

This sounds cynical, but it is mostly mechanical. A salesperson on commission will see opportunities the salaried account manager does not see. A board paid in equity reads the same balance sheet as a board paid in cash, and arrives at very different conclusions about acceptable risk.

I have stopped being surprised by this. The interesting question is no longer whether incentives shape decisions. They do. The interesting question is which incentives are operating on the people advising you, and whether anyone in the room has skin in the long-run version of the outcome.

The trick I use, when I am about to take advice that involves a decision I cannot undo, is to ask myself: who pays if this turns out to be wrong in three years, and who is paid for it being made today. If those are different people, I read the advice more carefully. Sometimes I take it anyway. Sometimes I do not. Either way, the question keeps me honest.

Smart investors and decent companies are not the ones who have escaped incentives. They are the ones who have made the incentives roughly point in the same direction as the long run.
