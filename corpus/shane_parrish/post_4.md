# The two kinds of meetings

There are meetings designed to make decisions, and meetings designed to communicate decisions. They look the same on the calendar. They require completely different preparation.

A decision meeting needs people who will be affected by the call, the relevant data, a written option set, and an explicit owner who is going to walk out and do the thing. If any of these are missing, the meeting cannot make a real decision; it can only feel like it did.

A communication meeting is for telling a group what was already decided, and answering questions about how it will be executed. It does not need full attendance. It does not need debate. It needs clarity.

Most meetings drift because they are run as a hybrid of the two, and people leave with different beliefs about what just happened. Some think a decision was made. Others think it was only floated. The work afterwards stalls, and people blame each other for being slow.

A useful rule for any leader: at the start of a meeting, name which kind it is. If you cannot, do not run the meeting. Reschedule it once the type is clear.

This is not a process improvement. It is the cheapest possible upgrade to organizational decision quality, and the one most consistently skipped.
