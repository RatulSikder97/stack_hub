# Reversibility is the underrated variable

When deciding how much rigor a decision needs, most people start with the size of the bet. How much money. How much time. How many people affected. These matter, but they are second-order. The first-order question is whether the decision is reversible.

A reversible decision can be made fast. If you are wrong, you fix it tomorrow, and the cost is mostly time. An irreversible decision needs to be made slowly, with explicit consideration of what you would do if it turns out to have been wrong, because the answer for that future-you is not "fix it tomorrow."

Most organizational decision-making errors I have seen come from one of two confusions. Reversible decisions made too slowly, because they sound expensive and trigger excessive caution. Or irreversible decisions made too fast, because they look procedurally similar to the reversible ones and got swept along in the same workflow.

The fix is a single question, asked early. "If we are wrong about this in a year, what does it cost us to undo?" If the answer is small, move now. If the answer is "we cannot undo it," slow down, write the analysis, and bring in a senior reviewer who can challenge it.

This is not a clever framework. It is a one-line filter that prevents most of the avoidable bad decisions I have watched smart people make.
